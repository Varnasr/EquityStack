Survey estimation

The part worth reading first

survey_estimation/

Design-based standard errors

Weighting is the half everyone remembers. A national household survey is also clustered, so an interval that ignores that is too narrow, often by half, and it errs in the direction that flatters your result. This does it properly, the same way Stata's svy: and R's survey do.

Worked example

NFHS-5 stunting, reproduced

Rebuilds India's published stunting table from the raw NFHS-5 files: 35.5 percent nationally, 46.1 in the poorest fifth falling to 22.9 in the richest. If your numbers match the published ones, your pipeline is sound. If they do not, you know before you publish rather than after.

Read the script →

Checked against R's survey package on the same data, to twelve significant figures. Three things it gets right that a hand-rolled version usually does not: a subgroup keeps the clusters it does not appear in, a small percentage never gets a negative lower bound, and the interval widens correctly when you have few clusters.

CleaningPython Get a messy export into shape, and keep a record of what you changed. ExplorationPython Look hard at a new dataset before you trust it with a finding. Impact evaluationPython Did the programme work? Difference-in-differences, matching and regression discontinuity. Survey estimationPython Get the confidence intervals right on NFHS, DHS or PLFS, where the sample is clustered. ModellingPython Check whether two of your predictors are telling you the same thing twice. ValidationPydantic Stop a bad column at the door, before it becomes a wrong number in someone's report. Input and outputPython Open a Stata or SPSS file, read a CSV too big for memory, hand a colleague a usable spreadsheet. Social sectorPython Build a health access index and compare districts on it. VisualisationPython District maps and annotated charts that can go straight into a report. NotebooksJupyter Two worked analyses: gender-disaggregated outcomes, and women's time use. Sample dataCSV Small datasets to try any of the above on. WorkflowsGuides Start-to-finish routes through an analysis, so you know what comes next.

Status

Stable

This repository works and is correct, but is not under active development. Bug reports are welcome and issues stay open; new features are unlikely, and replies are measured in weeks rather than days. Dependencies are pinned deliberately so that a clone still runs years from now. See the maintenance policy.

The companion repositories are InsightStack, which holds the calculators and the loaders that produce the data this reads, and FieldStack, which does the same work in R.
